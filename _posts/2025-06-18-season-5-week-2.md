This week each team tested its project against three questions. Do people want it? Will it create lasting value? Can we actually build it? This is the DVF framework: desirability, viability, and feasibility.

## How the projects stack up

**PECH** has an audience waiting: the people co-creating the project care a lot about the data. Nothing like it exists in Kitchener-Waterloo, or even across Canada, so plenty of people will be watching. The team found Ghost, an open source publishing platform that's easy to maintain and meets PECH's needs.

**One Million Neighbours** also has a built-in audience in the organizations taking part in its roundtables. It should interest residents and people thinking of moving here too. A few existing tools could be stitched together to build it, but the team is small right now.

**Project Ploughshares** mostly matters to the few people at Ploughshares who will use it, but it could create a lot of value for the wider community. It may be too ambitious for one season, so the team may break it into smaller projects across several seasons.

**Tiny Home Takeout** is very useful to Tiny Home Takeout and its community, though probably not to anyone else. The project is already well underway.

**Keystroke** is a malware detector that looks for one kind of malware, keyloggers, on your computer. It's a chance for its makers, and for CivicTechWR, to show what they can do.
