Season 8 started with two pitch nights, on May 6 and May 13. Here's what community members pitched.

## Returning projects

**LoMo** is a mutual aid app that connects people who need help with people offering it. After a season of work, the team wants to take it from prototype to something ready for a first group of 300 to 600 people. It most needs developers and designers.

**KW Accessible Housing Bridge** lists accessible rental units in Waterloo Region. This season the team wants to take it through user testing and get it ready to launch. It needs user researchers, front-end, back-end, and DevOps developers, writers for its resources, and testers.

**Waterloo Region Votes** is upgrading waterlooregionvotes.org and bringing it up to date for the 2026 election. It most needs people to reach out to community groups and to gather information for the site.

## New pitches

**Election widgets** would be small tools municipal candidates can add to their websites. Ideas include explaining what city and regional governments each do, showing where donations come from, collecting residents' concerns and ideas, and making decisions together.

**BookMyTherapy** would connect South Asian people in Canada with mental health services that fit their culture and are offered in several languages. It aims to be a low-stigma, low-barrier option at a time of long waitlists and high costs.

**Mosaic** would connect young people entering the job market with employers looking for particular skills, using small projects as a way to try working together first.

**Eviction prevention** would replace a huge spreadsheet the Social Development Centre uses to track people facing eviction as they move through its process. The goals are fewer mistakes and better data for the region.

**A meeting space booking platform** would list bookable community spaces and when they're free, all in one place. The City of Waterloo's Gathering Spaces Map lists venues and who to contact, but it doesn't offer an easy way to see availability or book.

**Canadaorganizes.ca**, from Indivisible, would help people across Canada find others taking part in non-violent, pro-democracy action near them. The team wants help with a postal code lookup, organizing resources, finding and creating events, hosting training, and linking to other tech tools.

**The Community Legal Information Project** would give people who can't easily get legal help clear information, not legal advice, so they can find their way through the justice system and make informed decisions. The team is interested in AI tools and data collection.
