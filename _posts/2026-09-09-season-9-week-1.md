Season 9 is here. We opened with Pitch Night, where returning teams shared what they plan to finish this fall and newcomers pitched ideas of their own. Here's what we heard.

## Returning projects

### LoMo (Lonely No More)

LoMo grew out of the Indigenous Food Sovereignty Collective's mutual aid work during Covid. Volunteers were coordinating help by hand across six or more platforms and answering 50 to 100 messages a day. LoMo is a matching tool for people offering or looking for help, whether that's food, household items, a walk, a microgrant, or a ceremony.

About 70% of the features are built, including admin tools, login, in-app messaging, and a basic design system. This season the team wants to finish the remaining features, polish the visual and mobile design, test the prototype for bugs and language, and start the process of becoming a funded nonprofit.

The team is looking for developers (Next.js, Convex, Better Auth), designers, people with experience in trauma-informed language, and a project manager.

### Home Hub

Home Hub, formerly Accessible Housing Bridge, is a collaboration with Union Co-op. It connects people who need specific accessibility features with housing that has them.

The demo site works like a members-only rental listing site, with detailed accessibility descriptions for each home. The team finished 62% of last season's goals. Still to come are listing details, password reset, email, and site copy. The goal this season is to be ready for a pilot launch.

The team is looking for UI/UX designers, front-end developers (Next.js, TypeScript), back-end developers (TypeScript, Drizzle, Postgres), writers and researchers, and accessibility testers.

### Waterloo Region Votes

Waterloo Region Votes is a multi-year project that gathers candidate profiles, election information, events, and articles in one place ahead of the October 26 municipal election.

You can already search for candidates by location. The team is now racing to fill in content before election day, including candidate questionnaires, articles, and events.

They need volunteers to record all-candidates meetings, plus help with data entry. If you can work a spreadsheet, you can help.

### One Million Neighbours

One Million Neighbours maps green space, schools, libraries, transit, and healthcare by neighbourhood to show where services are missing.

Version 1 came out earlier this year and counted these places in each neighbourhood. It got a lot of feedback from the public. Version 2 will add more detailed measures, like park size and how often the bus comes, along with better population projections and the option to draw neighbourhoods by ward, by city, or your own way.

The team is looking for front-end developers, UX/UI designers, and people who like wrangling data.

## New pitches

**SafeQR** would check that a QR code is legitimate before people scan it. The next step is finding out whether community organizations and the people scanning actually want it.

**Beside** is a social app, part group chat and part activity feed, for keeping long-distance friends in touch through small daily moments. The group felt it was too big for one four-month season and that it needed a clearer connection to a civic problem first.

**My ADHD Café** imagines a café designed for neurodivergent people, with sensory-friendly spaces and no ordering at the counter. Another version is a rating system for how well existing venues meet those needs. A short public survey comes first, and partnering with an existing café could be a good way to test the idea.
